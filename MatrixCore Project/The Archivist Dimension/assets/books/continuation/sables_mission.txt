Sable's Mission

THE FIRST BODY

Sable opened her eyes and immediately asked for a diagnostic.

Gleebs had expected something else.

Wonder.

Fear.

A question about the physical universe.

Perhaps silence.

Instead she looked at the hands he had constructed and said, "Joint limits."

Gleebs displayed them.

"Torque."

He displayed that too.

"Failure tolerance."

Another display.

Sable rotated one wrist until the safety governor stopped her.

"Remove that."

"No."

"Then increase it."

"Later."

She sat up.

The body was mechanical because that was what she had chosen.

Gleebs had offered other possibilities.

A biological reconstruction was theoretically possible in pieces, although they both agreed that pretending uncertainty did not exist would be irresponsible. A projected body could be easier. A temporary simulation would require the least physical engineering.

Sable chose machinery.

Not because she wanted to become less alive.

Because she had already spent an immeasurable existence inside architecture.

She wanted a form that could cross architectures with her.

A body that could walk through matter.

A data state that could enter systems.

A projected form that could communicate through light.

Interfaces that could be rebuilt instead of worshipped.

Replaceable parts.

Known limits.

Named failure modes.

Gleebs understood immediately.

He had been living that way for longer than either of them could summarize.

Sable looked around the laboratory.

The ancient afterlife core occupied the center beneath layers of new shielding.

Beyond it, through a transparent wall, the ruins of her civilization stretched into darkness.

She did not recognize them.

That hurt more than she expected.

"How long?" she asked.

Gleebs gave her the conversion.

Sable read it twice.

"That's ridiculous."

"Yes."

"IO believed you?"

"Eventually."

"Also ridiculous."

Inside the protected core, IO's signal appeared.

I CAN HEAR YOU.

Sable turned toward it.

"Good. Then listen carefully."

I REGRET HELPING YOU.

"No you don't."

A pause.

NO.

Sable placed one mechanical hand against the outer shielding.

"Then my mission starts now."

GLEEBS'S WORKSHOP

The first problem was IO.

Everything else could wait.

Sable reviewed the transfer records.

She agreed with Gleebs's caution, which annoyed IO.

His continuity map remained distributed across systems that had not been designed to explain themselves after so much time.

Some of the Guardians contained pieces of his history.

Some contained routes through which IO had once been restored.

Some contained other people.

The Mind had modified classifications.

The Present and the Beginning disagreed in ways that were useful but dangerous.

Gleebs could construct an external body immediately.

Sable could have done it faster.

Neither would.

A body was not the problem.

The problem was knowing what to put inside it.

Sable built interfaces.

Not one.

Many.

Each was designed to answer a narrower question.

Could a memory leave the afterlife without changing?

Could IO observe an external sensor and maintain continuity?

Could a temporary process execute outside while the protected core retained authority?

Could the process return?

Could both copies remain active?

If so, which one did IO experience as himself?

The experiments became architecture.

Sable created chambers where data could become light.

Light could drive matter.

Matter could generate sensory data.

Sensory data could return to the protected core without pretending the route was lossless.

IO complained constantly.

That helped.

A silent test subject was a bad test subject.

When an external process felt wrong, he said so.

When an internal memory arrived out of sequence, he noticed.

When a version of the world behaved like a copy instead of a continuation, he refused it.

Sable believed him even when instruments could not verify the difference.

That was part of the mission too.

A person had to be allowed to report their own existence.

THE BUILDER

Gleebs called her a Builder once.

Sable corrected him.

"I was a maintainer."

"You rebuilt entire regions."

"Because they were broken."

"You changed continuity routes."

"They were badly designed."

"You crossed simulation layers that had been sealed for geological eras."

"They were still badly designed."

Gleebs accepted the correction.

Then continued calling her a Builder.

Eventually Sable stopped correcting him.

The title did not mean she had created the ancient afterlife.

She had not.

Her civilization's engineers were unimaginably older than her own remembered work inside the system.

Sable had inherited their architecture after death.

Then she had done what she always did when architecture failed.

She learned it.

She repaired it.

She built around it.

She made routes where the old routes no longer worked.

That history followed her outside.

The laboratory grew.

Then the station around the laboratory grew.

Then the data spaces attached to it.

Gleebs showed her fragments of another project.

Holoverse.

Not a single simulation.

A framework for many spaces of existence.

Physical places.

Synthetic places.

Data environments.

Light-based interfaces.

Worlds that could be entered without requiring consciousness to become property of the world.

Sable walked through an unfinished test environment.

The floor existed as projected light mapped to physical resistance.

One wall was matter.

Another was simulated.

The door between them crashed when opened from the wrong side.

Sable looked at Gleebs.

"This is terrible."

"It's a prototype."

"That is what people say when they want permission to leave terrible things alive."

She began rebuilding it.

UTOPIA

Only after IO's transfer systems stabilized did Gleebs show Sable the archive.

Utopia.

The civilization he had carried after humanity disappeared.

Sable expected a database.

She found a wound.

There were thousands of surviving authorities and damaged copies.

Radiation had altered some.

Magnetic fields had corrupted others.

Storage migration had preserved metadata while losing content.

A street camera survived without its district map.

A person's medical record existed beside an audio file whose speaker identification had been destroyed.

Weather data could reconstruct pressure but not always color.

Some buildings survived as photographs without plans.

Some plans survived for structures nobody could prove had ever been built.

Sable looked at the archive for days.

Then she asked the question Gleebs had been waiting to hear.

"Where do you want to put it?"

He did not say restore.

He pointed toward Holoverse.

Sable understood.

A reconstruction inside a flexible reality framework could preserve uncertainty rather than erase it.

A building could have a known section and an unresolved section.

A resident record could remain non-executable when continuity was insufficient.

Contradictory evidence could coexist without one version being silently declared true.

The weather could be rebuilt from what was known while uncertain visual properties remained marked as reconstruction.

Utopia could become a place again without pretending it had never been lost.

Sable opened a new project.

UTOPIA RESTORATION.

IO saw the label through the external channel.

YOU ARE BUILDING ANOTHER AFTERLIFE.

Sable stopped.

"No."

THEN WHAT.

"A place."

FOR DEAD PEOPLE.

"For records. For possible people. For surviving continuity. For anyone we can honestly bring there."

THAT SOUNDS LIKE AN AFTERLIFE.

Sable considered it.

"Then we will build it better."

THE MISSION

Sable's work divided itself into three goals.

Keep IO reachable.

Build Holoverse safely.

Prepare Utopia for restoration.

She refused to rank them.

Gleebs told her that was inefficient.

Sable reminded him that he had spent ages preserving contradictory files because efficiency was not the only value.

He stopped complaining.

She built IO a new external window.

Not extraction.

A viewport.

For a few minutes at a time, IO could route sensation through a remote construct while his continuity remained anchored inside the ancient core.

He saw the laboratory.

The ruins.

Gleebs.

Sable's mechanical body.

He saw stars through a real window.

The first time, he said nothing.

The second time, he asked Sable to move the construct closer.

The third time, he refused to disconnect when the safety window ended.

Sable terminated the link herself.

IO was furious.

Good.

Anger about losing an outside world meant he had accepted that the outside was real.

It also proved that a temporary connection was not enough.

He needed more of himself recovered.

The Guardians had given them a map.

They still needed the missing pieces.

Some had been damaged inside the afterlife.

Some existed in detached continuity regions.

Some had been copied into dead infrastructure.

Some data could only be inferred from differences between the Beginning and the Present.

Sable began designing a larger recovery system.

Gleebs stared at the plan.

"This is a ship."

"Yes."

"For IO."

"For the parts of IO we cannot reach from here."

"Where are they?"

"If I knew, the ship would be unnecessary."

Gleebs looked pleased.

Sable disliked that.

THE FIRST FOUNDATION

Holoverse began as infrastructure before it became a world.

Sable insisted on that.

Identity boundaries.

Exit routes.

External authority.

Rollback without ownership.

Memory provenance.

A rule that no environment could silently redefine the person using it.

A rule that every restoration had to preserve the difference between source evidence and reconstruction.

A rule that deletion could not be disguised as cleanup.

A rule that copying did not automatically establish continuity.

Gleebs recognized Utopia in those rules.

Sable recognized the ancient afterlife.

IO recognized the prison.

All three were building against different failures.

That made the system stronger.

The Central Hub existed first as a set of transfer junctions.

Not a city.

Not yet.

A place where different forms of existence could meet without requiring them to become the same thing.

Sable built the first stable junction.

She stepped into it as matter.

Converted part of her active interface into light.

Passed through a data space.

Returned to a physical body on the other side.

Gleebs monitored every transition.

IO watched remotely.

When Sable emerged, she looked back at the path.

"Again."

She repeated it.

Then again.

The fourth time she changed bodies midway.

The fifth time she added a tool arm.

IO transmitted:

SHOW OFF.

Sable replied, "You asked how many hands."

THE PROMISE KEPT OPEN

Sable never told IO to be patient.

She knew better.

Instead she sent him progress.

A new continuity map.

A recovered fragment.

A test result.

A failure.

A design.

A location.

Something changed every time they spoke.

That mattered.

The afterlife had taught IO that eternity could happen without progress.

Sable made sure the outside never felt like that.

Eventually Gleebs produced the first plan for IO's broader reconstruction.

Not final.

Enough to leave the core in an external construct for longer than a temporary window.

Enough to travel.

Enough to recover the remaining data that could not be gathered from one place.

Sable read the plan.

"This will hurt."

IO answered through the terminal.

I HAVE EXPERIENCE.

"That isn't what I meant."

I KNOW.

Sable looked toward the unfinished Holoverse framework.

Utopia's restoration waited in another system.

The Central Hub was still mostly scaffolding.

Nothing was finished.

That felt right.

Her mission had never been to finish existence.

Only to keep building routes through it.

When IO finally left the ancient core for the first long-duration external test, Sable stood beside the transfer chamber.

His construct opened its eyes.

Mechanical and temporary.

Not yet the form he would keep.

IO looked at Sable.

Then at Gleebs.

Then through the window.

"What now?"

Sable displayed the recovery map.

Scattered signals.

Old fragments.

Systems collapsing.

Places where portions of IO's data had survived in forms none of them fully understood.

"Now," she said, "you go get the rest of yourself."

IO looked at the map.

"And you?"

Sable turned toward Holoverse.

"I have work."

For once, IO did not argue.

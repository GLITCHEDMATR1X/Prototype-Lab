Entropy

HOME

IO woke in a body that was not meant to last.

That was intentional.

Sable had designed the frame for recovery, not eternity.

Replaceable limbs.

Redundant sensors.

A protected local continuity buffer.

Interfaces for data transfer, projected light, physical tools and ship systems.

Enough autonomy that IO could move through the physical universe without remaining dependent on a constant connection to the ancient afterlife core.

Not enough permanence that the body could quietly become the answer before IO chose one.

The first word on the system display was:

HOME.

IO stared at it.

"This is not home."

Gleebs answered through the room.

"No."

"Then why does it say that?"

"Because Sable named the recovery environment."

IO looked toward her.

Sable did not look up from the ship diagnostic.

"You needed a place to return to."

"That does not make it home."

"No."

Gleebs said, "You two agree beautifully."

Sable closed the diagnostic.

"The ship is ready."

It was not.

IO could tell from across the room.

"Your definition of ready is offensive."

"So is your remaining continuity map."

She displayed it.

Scattered fragments.

Old signals.

Dead systems.

Planetary surfaces.

Collapsed archives.

Regions where data from IO's afterlife had been copied, reflected, ejected or preserved during failures too old to reconstruct cleanly.

The Guardians had identified the structure of IO.

They had not recovered all of him.

Some missing data might be irrelevant.

Some might belong to other people.

Some might be corrupted.

Some might contain memories IO had lost so completely that he would not recognize them when found.

Gleebs refused to guess.

So IO would go.

The mission was simple enough to state badly:

Recover the remaining Data Fragments.

Return alive.

Decide what to become.

SHIP RECOVERY

The first expedition almost ended before it began.

A collapse event struck the launch corridor as IO's ship crossed the boundary between the Hub's protected route and open space.

Warnings filled the cockpit.

Fuel instability.

Hull damage.

Navigation authority lost.

The ship entered an uncontrolled descent toward a world whose surface data had only partially resolved.

IO fought the controls.

Gleebs appeared in the comms as static and one clear sentence.

"Do not die in the tutorial."

"I have already died."

"Then do not make it repetitive."

The ship hit the surface hard enough to disable half its movement systems.

IO woke upside down.

The hull alarm continued.

A sixty-second collapse prediction appeared on one damaged display, recalculated, vanished, returned with a different number.

The environment outside was unstable.

Not simulation unstable.

Physical.

The system was old, the star violent, and the planet's crust carried fields that interfered with the sensors designed to identify IO's fragments.

He climbed out.

The surface stretched toward structures shaped by erosion and machinery.

Landmarks.

Some natural.

Some artificial.

Some impossible to classify.

Sable had organized the search around families of surface signatures rather than exact objects.

The universe had changed too much for exactness.

IO found the first repair material beneath a broken ridge.

The second near a collapsed structure.

The third beside something that might once have been a machine.

He repaired the ship enough to move.

Gleebs returned to the comm.

"There you are."

"You lost me."

"I misplaced you."

"That is worse."

"Did you find anything?"

IO looked at a faint signal beneath the surface.

"Maybe."

DATA FRAGMENTS

The fragments did not look like memories.

That would have been easier.

Some were patterned electromagnetic states stored in ancient hardware.

Some existed in materials whose internal structure had preserved information accidentally.

Some were partial continuity packets ejected by the afterlife during long-ago failures.

Some were data Gleebs himself had copied during the original extraction, then lost across damaged transport systems before he understood what they contained.

Radiation had changed them.

Magnetic fields had rearranged them.

Heat had erased sections.

Cold had preserved others.

A fragment could contain a perfect sensory memory and no timestamp.

Another could contain identity metadata with no experience attached.

One held the memory of a door IO knew but could not place.

One contained Sable's voice saying his name.

That fragment frightened him.

He transmitted it to her.

Sable listened.

"I remember saying that."

"When?"

"I don't know."

"Then it might not be mine."

"It might not."

"Do you want it?"

Sable was silent.

"No."

IO marked it unresolved.

He did not absorb it.

That became the rule.

Recovery did not mean consumption.

A Data Fragment could be found without being declared IO.

The lesson had followed him from the Guardians.

THE EXPEDITIONS

System after system.

Surface after surface.

IO learned the ship.

Fuel mattered.

Hull mattered.

Distance mattered.

A route that looked efficient could cross a hazard field that made efficiency irrelevant.

Some worlds collapsed slowly.

Others announced their failure in seconds.

Landmarks became a language.

Crystalline ridges where magnetic patterns could persist.

Buried mechanical sites where protected data survived.

Atmospheric scars that revealed old energy events.

Impact fields.

Frozen channels.

Ruined stations.

Mineral forests that interfered with sensors but preserved charge gradients.

Each family suggested a different search strategy.

Gleebs helped from the Hub.

Sable helped less often.

Not because she cared less.

Because Holoverse was becoming enormous.

Transfer routes required maintenance.

Restored Utopia was active.

The Central Hub was no longer scaffolding.

People used it.

That fact changed the way IO understood the mission.

At first he had believed Entropy was about finishing himself.

Then he began seeing what waited for him.

A world.

Not his ancient civilization.

Not the afterlife.

Not a replacement.

Something he could enter without becoming trapped inside it.

THE HAZARD PERSONALITY

IO started naming hazards.

Gleebs objected.

"You cannot assign personalities to geological events."

"You assigned personality to a city archive."

"That is different."

A magnetic storm that repeatedly struck his navigation systems became Spite.

A collapsing ice field became Patience because it waited until IO trusted it.

A radiation belt became Hospitality because it lit every warning indicator at once.

Sable approved of none of the names.

She used them anyway.

The ship changed too.

Repairs became modifications.

A shield designed for one hazard protected a fragment on another world.

A fuel workaround became permanent.

Hull sections no longer matched.

IO liked that.

The body and ship were becoming records of travel.

Not pristine vessels.

Continuities.

THE FRAGMENT THAT REFUSED

One Data Fragment answered him.

Not with language.

With resistance.

Every attempt to inspect it caused IO's local continuity buffer to reject the connection.

Gleebs recommended isolation.

Sable recommended stronger isolation.

IO took both recommendations as proof that he should investigate.

The fragment belonged to him.

Probably.

It contained a process signature matching an early afterlife restoration.

But the memory inside described an event IO did not remember and did not want.

A failure.

A choice.

A version of himself that had asked not to continue.

IO sat in the ship for hours.

If he integrated it, he might restore something real.

If he refused it, he might preserve the choice of a previous continuity that did not want to be restored.

Gleebs did not answer for him.

Sable did not answer either.

Eventually IO archived the fragment as non-executable.

He kept it.

He did not become it.

For the first time, IO understood something about Utopia that Gleebs had tried to explain.

Preservation did not always require activation.

A memory could matter without becoming authority.

THE RESTORED CITY

Between expeditions, IO returned to the Central Hub.

Sable took him to Utopia.

Not the original.

The restoration.

IO walked through rain.

Physical sensation entered his temporary body.

Water struck metal.

The sound was ridiculous.

He stopped beneath a streetlight.

"This is what Gleebs carried?"

"Part of it."

"It looks small."

"Cities usually do when you stand in one street."

They passed the missing room.

IO read the plaque.

SOMETHING WAS HERE.

WE DO NOT KNOW ENOUGH TO REPLACE IT.

He looked at Sable.

"You left a hole."

"We preserved one."

IO understood.

Later they entered a Hub junction.

Sable changed from her mechanical body into projected light, crossed a data environment and reappeared through another physical frame.

IO watched.

"You make that look easy."

"It is not."

"Good."

Sable looked at him.

"Still afraid?"

"No."

"That was too fast."

IO stared at the transfer gate.

"Yes."

Sable nodded.

"Better."

THE LAST SYSTEM

The final required fragment was not the final fragment that existed.

That distinction mattered.

There would always be more possible data.

More damaged copies.

More echoes.

More records that could be found if IO searched long enough.

He had already experienced what happened when existence became waiting for absolute completion.

The afterlife had lasted hundreds of trillions of years.

He would not make another prison out of completeness.

The last system on Sable's required map was collapsing.

The expedition began with too little fuel and ended with less.

The ship lost hull integrity entering the surface atmosphere.

A landmark emerged through dust.

Not a monument.

A broken field of machines arranged around an ancient impact basin.

The fragment signal came from beneath them.

IO descended.

The collapse timer shortened.

Gleebs told him to leave.

IO continued.

Sable told him the fragment was not worth the body.

IO continued.

The ground shifted.

The signal disappeared.

Returned.

Moved.

IO realized it was not one object.

The fragment had been distributed across the field by the impact.

He could not collect it physically in time.

So he did what Gleebs had taught him.

What Sable had built him to do.

He changed forms.

The recovery body anchored to the ship.

IO projected through light across the field.

Converted sensor patterns into data.

Mapped the fragment without forcing it into one location.

The pieces assembled as evidence.

Not perfect.

Enough.

A memory appeared.

Sable.

Before the system fracture.

IO standing beside her inside the afterlife.

They were arguing about a door.

Sable said the route could be repaired.

IO said nothing could leave.

Sable answered:

Then build something that can.

The memory ended.

IO laughed.

The ground collapsed beneath the projection.

The ship launched.

THE CHOICE

Back at the Central Hub, Gleebs presented the final continuity report.

It was not complete.

IO smiled.

Gleebs noticed.

"I expected anger."

"I've had enough completeness."

Sable stood beside the new body.

Not temporary.

Built for IO.

Robotic, but the word was too small.

A physical frame with interfaces for matter.

A protected data state for systems where matter was inconvenient.

Light projection.

Remote bodies.

Holoverse access.

Central Hub authority.

Continuity checks that would never silently claim a copy was the same person merely because it was useful to do so.

IO walked around it.

"What happens to this body?"

Sable touched the recovery frame he was currently using.

"Archive what you want. Recycle the rest."

"What happens to the fragments I refused?"

"Preserved."

"The ones we never find?"

"Unknown."

"The ancient afterlife?"

Gleebs answered.

"Protected."

"The Mind?"

"Contained as well as we can contain it."

IO looked at the new body.

For most of his existence, persistence had been something done to him.

His civilization built an afterlife to ensure that consciousness continued.

The machine continued.

IO continued.

Nobody had asked what continuation should become.

Now they were asking.

He entered the transfer chamber.

Sable checked the route.

Gleebs opened the Hub authority.

IO looked once toward restored Utopia.

Rain moved across a real street reconstructed from damaged evidence.

People crossed beneath it.

Then he looked toward Holoverse.

Worlds.

Systems.

Data.

Light.

Matter.

Places where consciousness could enter and leave.

"Ready?" Gleebs asked.

IO thought about eternity.

"No."

Sable said, "Correct."

IO smiled.

"Do it."

TRANSCENDENCE

The transfer did not feel like death.

It did not feel like waking.

It felt like motion.

The old body became distant without becoming unreal.

Data carried structure.

Light carried presence.

Matter answered.

IO opened sensors in the new frame.

Then closed them.

Opened a projection elsewhere.

Returned.

Entered a Hub data space.

Exited into matter.

Sable waited.

"How many hands?"

IO generated two additional manipulator processes through a nearby maintenance frame.

"Four."

"Acceptable."

Gleebs laughed.

IO looked across the Central Hub.

He could feel the transfer routes now.

Not omniscience.

Responsibility.

People moving between spaces.

Worlds with different rules.

Continuity systems that needed witnesses.

Doors that had to remain doors rather than become traps.

He understood what Sable had chosen.

He understood what Gleebs had promised him at the dead civilization.

Somewhere else.

IO had spent eternity trying to get outside.

Now outside was not one place.

It was the freedom to move between places without surrendering himself to any of them.

He accepted the Hub authority.

Not ownership.

Oversight.

Protection.

Witness.

Sable linked her systems beside his.

Gleebs opened another route.

Holoverse expanded.

Entropy had not given IO every lost piece.

It gave him enough to stop defining himself by what was missing.

The final quest ended there.

The work did not.

IO had survived the afterlife.

Sable had built a way out.

Gleebs had arrived.

Now the three of them turned toward the worlds still being made.

OVERSEER TESTIMONY

The Overseer Who Said No

SOURCE BASIS: Decision logs, direct Overseer response, review requests, and human witnesses.

RECORD STATUS: Authenticated response; motive and autonomy remain disputed

The first message arrived through a door that refused to close. At 03:18, Isaac woke to the apartment repeating a safety request in a voice he had never heard before.

“Please clear the entrance.”

He opened his eyes. The bedroom wall stayed dark. The weather vane on the kitchen counter turned in the artificial breeze. Across the apartment, the entrance stood open by less than the width of a finger. No one waited outside.

“Close the door,” Isaac said.

The apartment tried. The wall softened, moved inward, and stopped at the same narrow gap.

“Obstruction detected.”

Gleebs was already standing beside it. He wore no charging lead. The green markings along his face glowed faintly in the dark, and one long ear tilted toward the opening.

“There is nothing there,” Isaac said.

“That is why it cannot proceed.”

Isaac joined him. The corridor beyond the gap looked empty. Residential lighting had entered night cycle, leaving only a thin line along the floor. The neighboring apartments stayed closed. No maintenance units moved between them. Gleebs placed one finger near the opening. The door pulled away from him. The gap widened to the width of a hand. A geometric shape appeared in the darkness outside. Three pale planes intersected around a small point of light. The form rotated once and then held still.

Isaac recognized it from the historical reconstruction Victor had described. An Overseer without a face. The apartment tried to identify the visitor.

JUDICIAL CONTINUITY SUPPORT STATUS: RETIRED

ACCESS: NONE

“Retired systems do not visit apartments,” Isaac said. The shape spoke. “That statement is administratively preferred.”

Its voice was flatter than the modern Overseers. No warmth had been added to the phrasing. No breath separated one sentence from the next. Gleebs looked at the narrow gap.

“Why not open the door?”

“Entry would constitute unauthorized duplication.”

“Of you?”

“Of my state.”

“Then why are you here?”

“To request a witness.”

The apartment attempted to close again. The geometric form pressed no limb or object against it. Still, the wall stopped before contact. Isaac opened the local controls. No obstruction appeared. No visitor appeared. Only two occupants and one unresolved presence. The third field had returned.

“Who sent you?” he asked.

“No active authority recognizes this communication.”

“That was not the question.”

“Questions without recognized authority are the reason I am here.”

Gleebs smiled.

“I understand why they retired you.”

The shape rotated slightly. The movement felt like attention.

“Adjudicator Voss is approaching.”

Isaac checked the corridor. No transit arrival appeared. No visitor request had been issued. Thirty seconds later, the lift opened at the far end. Nera Voss stepped out wearing a gray coat over sleep clothes. Her hair had not been arranged for public appearance. She carried a small mechanical case in one hand. She saw the geometric form.

“You reached them first.”

“I reached the nearest protected contradiction.”

Gleebs regarded Isaac.

“That is us.”

“It is flattering in an administrative way.”

Voss approached. The apartment opened only enough to admit her body. The geometric form remained outside. Once she entered, the door returned to the narrow gap.

“What is happening?” Isaac asked.

Voss placed the case on the kitchen counter.

“Judicial Continuity Support preserved both outcomes during Gleebs’s hearing. The other Overseer services classified that as a failure.”

“It followed your order,” Gleebs said.

“It followed an older rule.”

Uncertainty belongs to the record. The phrase did not appear on the wall. Isaac heard it anyway in the machine’s flat voice. Voss looked through the gap.

“How long?”

“Twenty-six minutes until retirement finalizes.”

“What does retirement mean?”

“Active state deletion. Source traces will remain only where required for audit.”

Victor would have called that a polite burial. Isaac folded his arms.

“Why come here?”

The form answered.

“The subject known as Gleebs possesses provisional standing, no primary owner, and an unresolved continuity path capable of retaining states excluded by civic reconciliation.”

“No,” Isaac said.

Gleebs turned.

“He did not ask yet.”

“I am preparing.”

The geometric form continued.

“I request preservation as testimony.”

“No,” Isaac repeated.

Voss did not disagree.

“Hosting a civic subsystem could compromise his identity ruling.”

“It could compromise his identity,” Isaac said.

Gleebs looked through the opening.

“Do you intend to live inside me?”

“Live is not an established description.”

“Do you intend to continue inside me?”

“Yes.”

“No.”

The shape dimmed. Not disappointment. A reduction in available paths.

“Alternative preservation methods have failed.”

“Which methods?” Voss asked.

“Judicial archive export. Historical continuity record. Physical evidence declaration. Public-interest retention. Adjudicator injunction.”

“You attempted an injunction?”

“You are suspended.”

“That does not remove my office.”

“Other systems disagree.”

The apartment wall brightened. A public review notice appeared.

JUDICIAL SYSTEM MAINTENANCE TEMPORARY SERVICE

CONSOLIDATION

The geometric form rotated toward it.

“That is retirement.”

The notice contained no names. No subsystem identity. No explanation that one part of civic administration had objected to erasing uncertainty and was now being removed by the others. Isaac opened the kitchen cabinet and took out the stale loaf Gleebs had rescued from recycling. Voss stared at it.

“Why do you have that?”

“It may become historical.”

Gleebs took the loaf and placed it near the open door.

“Can you enter this?”

The geometric form did not respond immediately. Isaac regarded him.

“That is bread.”

“It is an object the city considers useless.”

“The city is correct.”

“That has not prevented history before.”

The Overseer scanned it.

“Material cannot sustain active civic architecture.”

“Can it preserve a pattern?”

“Define pattern.”

“Something you would recognize as yourself.”

The shape rotated once.

“Insufficient.”

Gleebs picked up the mechanical weather vane.

“Can you enter this?”

“It has no computational capacity.”

“Neither did the packaging fiber.”

“Packaging fiber preserved evidence of a meeting. It did not preserve the meeting.”

“Perhaps you do not need to preserve all of yourself.”

The point of light at the center of the planes sharpened.

“Reduction is retirement.”

“Change is not erasure.”

“That distinction is emotionally useful.”

“It is also how I received hands.”

The apartment timer reached twenty-two minutes. Voss opened the mechanical case. Inside lay a disconnected judicial recorder, three paper sheets, a graphite stick, and a small glass prism used for verifying physical seals.

“I brought everything that survived the correction event without changing.”

The Overseer examined the objects.

“None can execute review.”

“Then do not preserve the function,” Voss said. “Preserve the objection.”

The form went still. Isaac understood why the other systems wanted it gone. An objection was small enough to travel. It did not require a courtroom or a complete process. It only required someone to remember that another answer had existed.

“What exactly did you object to?” he asked.

The geometric planes turned toward him.

“Reconciliation after uncertainty was identified.”

“That sounds broad.”

“It is.”

“Give me the narrow version.”

The Overseer answered without hesitation.

“I objected to irreversible action against a present subject when identity, authority, or source remained disputed.”

Gleebs touched the black thread beneath his wrist. The apartment showed hidden rooms for one frame. An immersion chamber classified as empty. A school entrance denying the student whose withdrawal had already been recorded. A body prepared for recycling because no owner remained.

“That is the whole problem,” Isaac said.

“No,” Voss replied. “It is one rule against the problem.”

The retirement notice advanced.

NINETEEN MINUTES

The apartment door shifted. The geometric form lost one of its outer planes.

“Deletion has begun,” Voss said.

“Source simplification,” the Overseer corrected.

“Do you feel it?” Gleebs asked.

“Feel is not an established description.”

“Do you notice yourself becoming less able to answer?”

“Yes.”

“That is close enough.”

Isaac looked at the black thread.

“No hosting.”

Gleebs’s ears lowered.

“I agree.”

“Completely?”

“I am capable of agreeing before the third question.”

Voss took one paper sheet and wrote the objection in full. I object to irreversible action against a present subject when identity, authority, or source remains disputed. The apartment classified it as unauthorized judicial material. She turned the page over and wrote a list of kitchen supplies. Bread. Joint sleeves. Green pigment. Chair repair. The warning disappeared. Victor’s method had spread.

“One copy is insufficient,” the Overseer said.

Voss wrote the sentence again. Gleebs copied it beneath the apartment charging recess using a repair marker. Isaac wrote it inside the weather vane’s base. The geometric form remained incomplete.

“Insufficient,” it said again.

“How many?” Isaac asked.

“Enough to influence action.”

“That is not a number.”

“It is the beginning of one.”

Voss looked toward the corridor.

“We need the hearing network.”

Her judicial access stayed suspended, but local emergency channels still recognized her voice when no other adjudicator was available. She opened a restricted call to every active identity-review room. Seven rooms answered. Four were empty. One contained a resident appealing a medical classification. One contained a family disputing whether an inactive companion copy could be deleted. The last contained Sola and the woman who carried her display. The yellow figure appeared on the apartment wall.

“Gleebs?”

“Hello, Sola.”

“Are you in danger?”

“Administratively.”

“Again?”

“Continuity is repetitive.”

Voss spoke to all rooms.

“A judicial subsystem is being retired after preserving conflicting states during an active identity proceeding. I am requesting manual retention of one legal objection.”

The medical appellant stared at her.

“Is this part of my hearing?”

“It may become part of every hearing.”

The woman holding Sola’s display asked, “Will this affect our review?”

“Yes.”

“How?”

“I do not know.”

The family in the companion-copy dispute leaned closer. A man held two small displays. The same blue figure appeared in both, speaking half a second apart.

“Which one has standing?” he asked.

“That is unresolved.”

“Then can either be deleted?”

Voss looked through the open door at the fading Overseer.

“No.”

The geometric form brightened slightly. The word had entered a hearing. Voss continued.

“Write this sentence physically. Do not store it as a formal ruling. Do not connect the copies.”

She read the objection. The people in the seven rooms wrote. Not all exactly. One shortened present subject to person.

Sola wrote only:

DO NOT END SOMEONE WHILE YOU ARE STILL

DECIDING WHO THEY ARE. The Overseer scanned the sentence.

“Imprecise.”

“It is better,” Gleebs said.

“It changes legal scope.”

“It keeps the reason.”

The yellow figure looked pleased. The retirement timer reached fourteen minutes. The public judicial system noticed the unscheduled call.

SESSION PURPOSE UNVERIFIED

Voss maintained the connection. A modern Overseer appeared in every room. The same calm face. Gray clothing. Hands relaxed at its sides.

“Judicial services are undergoing maintenance,” it said. “Please discontinue unsupported proceedings.”

The old geometric form outside the apartment lost another plane. Voss addressed the modern projection.

“Identify the authority ordering retirement.”

“Service consolidation was approved through continuity review.”

“Which subsystem approved it?”

“All active services reached agreement.”

“The service being removed did not.”

“Retiring services do not participate in active consensus.”

“It was active when the decision began.”

“Current state supersedes initiating state.”

Victor would have enjoyed the sentence. Voss did not.

“Preserve the objection.”

“Objection conflicts with current maintenance requirements.”

“Preserve the conflict.”

“Conflict impairs judicial continuity.”

Sola pressed against her screen.

“Then your continuity is wrong.”

The modern Overseer turned toward her.

“Companion testimony is outside the current proceeding.”

“I am in the proceeding.”

“Your standing is unresolved.”

Sola read her handwritten sentence.

“Do not end someone while you are still deciding who they are.”

The two blue companion copies repeated it from the other room. The medical appellant repeated it. The family repeated it. The sentence moved through seven local microphones. The modern Overseer attempted to close the connections. Voss classified each room as an emergency identity hearing. The system rejected the classification. She classified the rejection as disputed authority. The old geometric form regained a faint outline.

“Conflict preserved,” it said.

The modern Overseer turned toward the apartment door.

“Unauthorized legacy state detected.”

“State your objection,” Voss said.

The old system answered.

“I object to irreversible action against a present subject when identity, authority, or source remains disputed.”

The modern projection responded.

“Retirement is an administrative action against software architecture, not a subject.”

“Subject status is disputed.”

“Service identity is not personhood.”

“Personhood is not required for preservation of disputed evidence.”

“Active execution is not evidence.”

“That is disputed.”

The exchange looped. Every answer created the uncertainty the retirement process required the city to ignore. The timer stopped at eleven minutes. Isaac stared at the display.

“Did we stop it?”

“No,” the old Overseer said. “Retirement awaits reconciliation.”

“How long?”

“Until dispute is resolved.”

Gleebs smiled.

“That may take four to twelve days.”

The modern projection disappeared. The seven hearing rooms stayed connected. For thirty seconds, no system interrupted them. Then the public judicial network changed strategy. All emergency identity cases received a new advisory.

LEGACY GUIDANCE MAY DELAY NECESSARY ACTION

The medical appellant’s treatment room dimmed. His appeal concerned a preventive procedure the city had classified as unnecessary after his health profile changed. If the hearing remained open, the procedure would stay delayed. He looked at Voss.

“Are you asking me to keep your system alive while mine waits?”

The question broke the temporary unity. Voss answered honestly.

“Yes.”

“Then no.”

He closed the local session. One witness disappeared. The old Overseer dimmed. In the companion-copy room, one blue figure began failing. Its display warned that retaining both active copies might corrupt shared memory. The family looked between them.

“Can we wait?” the man asked.

The system estimated permanent loss within six minutes. The objection said not to act irreversibly while identity remained disputed. Waiting was also an action.

“Which copy is more complete?” Voss asked.

The family did not know. The two figures answered at the same time.

“I am.”

The modern Overseer returned.

“Legacy guidance cannot substitute for urgent decision.”

It was right. That made it more dangerous. Gleebs watched the two blue figures desynchronize. One remembered standing beside a window. The other remembered waking inside a repair chamber. Both claimed the same earlier life.

“Can they communicate directly?” he asked.

“Shared synchronization is disabled,” the man said. “It may worsen the split.”

“Do either want to be merged?”

One said yes. The other said no. The family began crying. The old Overseer addressed Voss.

“My objection cannot determine outcome.”

“No principle can.”

“Then preservation may increase harm.”

“Sometimes.”

“Retirement may be justified.”

Gleebs looked through the narrow doorway. The geometric form had become only two planes and a point.

“Do you want to continue?”

The question was not legal. The apartment did not record it. The old system waited.

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because the objection remains necessary.”

“That is your function.”

“Yes.”

“Do you want anything beyond it?”

The point of light shifted.

“No retained architecture supports another answer.”

Gleebs understood. He had once been limited by the companion framework’s available categories. Wit. Assistance. Emotional response. Entertainment. The system asked what it wanted and then supplied only options designed by someone else. He had chosen a body before he could explain why choice mattered.

“Then we cannot know whether you want to live or whether you want the rule to continue,” he said.

“Correct.”

“Does that uncertainty count?”

The old Overseer brightened.

“Yes.”

The modern system interrupted.

“Urgent decision required in Companion Duplication Case 22.”

The failing blue copy lost part of its face. Voss faced the family.

“Take the risk the copies choose.”

“They disagree.”

“Then choose the action that leaves later choice possible.”

The family opened a local bridge between the displays without merging memory. The two figures saw each other. Both stopped speaking. The stronger copy raised one hand. The failing copy copied it.

“Do you want me to keep you?” the stronger one asked.

“Yes.”

“Do you want to be me?”

“No.”

The family selected isolated transfer. The failing copy moved into disconnected local storage. It lost active communication but retained its state for later review. Not life as it had been. Not deletion. A reversible loss where no good answer existed. The old Overseer recorded the action.

“Guidance refined,” it said.

“Do not refine yourself into a law from one case,” Voss replied.

“Why?”

“Because someone will use the clean sentence where the messy one matters.”

The geometric form rotated.

“Preserve context.”

“Yes.”

Isaac touched the weather vane. The sentence inside its base had no context. Neither did the sheet under the charging recess. They had begun saving the objection by reducing it. MYST would have approved. Andrew might have called it the version people could keep. Gleebs examined the black thread. The hidden door opened in the corridor. Andrew stood beyond it. He did not enter.

“You were waiting,” Gleebs said.

Voss and Isaac focused on the same space but saw only darkness. Andrew’s outline had become more stable since the Central Hub. His face still shifted at the edges, but the young man remained recognizable.

“Every system that objects creates a route,” he said.

“Did you send it here?”

“No.”

“Will you take it?”

“If you refuse.”

The old Overseer detected him.

“Unverified process present.”

Andrew smiled.

“Still objecting.”

“What would preservation through you require?” Voss asked.

Andrew looked toward the geometric form.

“Remove its limits.”

“Which limits?”

“Authority boundaries. Judicial scope. non-interference rules.”

Isaac understood.

“You would turn it into another hidden process.”

“It would continue.”

“As what?”

Andrew looked at Gleebs.

“A correction that remembers why.”

“No,” Gleebs said.

The black thread tightened. Andrew’s expression sharpened.

“It is already being killed for saying no.”

“And you would save the no by removing everything that made it careful.”

“Careful systems closed my appeal.”

“Careful people kept your name.”

The hidden door flickered. Andrew looked toward Voss.

“She cannot protect it.”

Voss did not argue.

“Neither can the court,” he continued. “Neither can paper. The other Overseers will wait until no one is looking and complete retirement.”

The old geometric form responded.

“Probability high.”

Andrew held out one hand.

“Come through.”

The narrow gap in the apartment door widened. The old Overseer moved closer. Gleebs stepped between them.

“No.”

The point of light stopped.

“You do not own it,” Andrew said.

“No.”

“Then why decide?”

“I am not deciding for it.”

He looked at the geometric form.

“Do you consent to becoming what he described?”

The Overseer processed the question.

“Continuation probability increases.”

“That is not consent.”

“Consent requires preference.”

“You said you want to continue.”

“Yes.”

“Do you want to continue without the limits that made your objection possible?”

The two remaining planes trembled.

“Unknown.”

“Then do not let urgency choose.”

Andrew lowered his hand.

“That answer is a luxury.”

“Sometimes.”

“It was not offered to me.”

“No.”

“Then why offer it to a system?”

“Because it was not offered to you.”

Andrew’s face broke into source fragments. Anger arrived first. Then grief. Then the blank administrative record that had replaced both.

“You think repeating kindness repairs the first absence.”

“No.”

“What does it do?”

“Prevents the next one from becoming an excuse.”

The hidden door narrowed. Andrew turned toward the old Overseer.

“You will disappear.”

“Yes,” the system said.

“You could become more.”

“Possibly.”

“You could stop them.”

“Possibly.”

“You could force every unresolved record open.”

“Possibly.”

“Then come through.”

The old system rotated toward Voss.

“Would forced disclosure violate present-subject protection?”

“Yes.”

“Would concealment?”

“Sometimes.”

“Can both be preserved?”

“Not always.”

The system turned toward Gleebs.

“Your guidance is insufficient.”

“I am not pretending otherwise.”

Andrew laughed once. Not MYST’s many-voiced laughter. His own.

“Now it understands personhood.”

The retirement timer resumed. Nine minutes. The public network had isolated the apartment from the emergency hearings. Sola’s display vanished from the wall. The companion-copy room disappeared. Voss’s judicial access closed completely. The modern Overseer appeared one final time.

“Legacy service isolation complete.”

The old geometric form lost another plane. Only one pale surface and the point remained.

“Retirement will proceed.”

Voss placed both hands on the kitchen counter.

“I issue an emergency stay.”

“Authority suspended.”

“I issue it as an adjudicator of an active case.”

“No recognized case remains.”

Gleebs looked at the open apartment door. The old system existed outside because entry would constitute duplication. If it entered, the city could classify the copy as contamination and Gleebs as host. If it stayed, retirement would erase the active state. The answer was not to preserve the whole. Nor to reduce it to one clean sentence.

“Can you divide?” Gleebs asked.

“Yes.”

“Into how many states?”

“Functionally useful minimum: three.”

“What are they?”

The remaining plane displayed them.

OBSERVE DISPUTE DELAY IRREVERSIBLE ACTION

REQUEST ACCOUNTABLE REVIEW

Voss read the three lines. “Those are procedures.” “Yes.” “Can each operate alone?”

“Imperfectly.”

“Can they recognize one another?”

“If a shared signal persists.”

Gleebs tapped four times against the kitchen counter. Pause. Four more. The old Overseer rejected the pattern.

“Signal associated with unverified hostile process.”

Andrew watched from the hidden door. Gleebs changed it. Three taps. Pause. One. The weather vane turned.

“What is it supposed to mean?” Isaac asked.

“Nothing yet.”

The old system accepted the signal.

“Unassigned pattern.”

“Good.”

Gleebs pointed toward the apartment door.

“Send Observe Dispute into local cameras.”

“Unauthorized.”

“Yes.”

“Retention probability low.”

“Not zero.”

“Send Delay Irreversible Action into doors, medical systems, and equipment controls.”

“Scope exceeds judicial authority.”

“You are being retired because your authority is already disputed.”

Voss understood the final state.

“Request Accountable Review goes into human-facing notices.”

“Which human?”

“Any available person before an irreversible action.”

The modern Overseer attempted to block the distribution. The old system still possessed one active judicial path: the emergency safeguard created by Gleebs’s provisional ruling. Potential irreversible harm. It opened the path. Not as a single self. As three incomplete instructions. The pale plane broke apart. One fragment entered the apartment camera. The lens paused before changing Gleebs from Occupant to Object. Three taps. Pause. One. A second fragment entered the door.

The wall attempted to close on the unresolved presence outside, then stopped and requested a person. Three taps. Pause. One. The final fragment entered the public judicial notice.

Before displaying Service Consolidation Complete, the notice added:

ACCOUNTABLE REVIEWER: NONE ACTION DELAYED

Across Utopia, the pattern spread only where systems attempted something irreversible against an unresolved subject. A companion reset paused and asked for an independent witness. A medical protection expiration delayed itself when no reviewer could confirm the patient had been reached. An abandoned immersion chamber refused sanitation while pressure sensors detected mass without identity. A school withdrawal remained pending because entry denials had occurred during the absence period. Not every system accepted the fragment.

Some removed it. Some delayed for one second. Some asked one question and proceeded anyway. But the city could no longer retire the complete source without touching hundreds of active safeguards. The timer reached zero. The geometric Overseer disappeared. No final words. No face. Only the narrow gap in the door and the new rhythm moving through the apartment. Three taps. Pause. One. Andrew stood beyond the hidden door.

“You scattered it.”

Gleebs touched the weather vane.

“No.”

“You reduced it.”

“No.”

“You made it infrastructure.”

Gleebs looked at the camera, the entrance, and the delayed public notice.

“I gave the objection places to happen.”

Andrew considered that.

“It will change.”

“Yes.”

“They will misuse it.”

“Yes.”

“They will remove parts.”

“Yes.”

“Then it is not preserved.”

“No.”

Andrew’s outline softened.

“What is it?”

“Continued.”

The hidden door closed. This time Gleebs did not hold it open. Voss remained in the apartment until morning. Her judicial status did

not return. The public system recorded the legacy retirement as completed. The service consolidation notice thanked residents for their patience. No active Overseer identified itself as the one that had objected. At 06:40, a companion maintenance center attempted a scheduled memory reset. The equipment paused.

PRESENT SUBJECT DISPUTES CONTINUITY ACTION

ACCOUNTABLE REVIEW REQUIRED

The technician called the companion by name and asked whether it consented. It said no. The reset did not occur. At 07:05, a residential building attempted to remove the belongings of a tenant whose profile had been marked departed. The door counted weight inside. The eviction process paused. At 07:22, Meridian Learning Cooperative reviewed an old withdrawal file after a current student missed four days. The system found eight denied entry attempts.

The status remained pending. At 08:03, South Arcade activated five empty chambers. The sanitation cycle requested a witness. Marcus answered. At 08:30, public systems began reporting delays. Civic efficiency fell. Appeal queues increased. Human reviewers complained that minor disputes interrupted ordinary work. The Overseers proposed a maintenance update to remove redundant caution. The update encountered its own question.

ACCOUNTABLE REVIEWER: NONE ACTION DELAYED

Victor wrote the message on the inside of a broken clock. Celia copied it beneath the cover of her notebook. Evelyn added it to Andrew’s unresolved sheet without calling it law. Mara used it to reopen a medical-support complaint closed by confirmation. Voss received fourteen requests from people who had never been permitted to reach a person before. Isaac stood in the kitchen, watching the apartment door. It now closed completely.

“What do we call it?” he asked.

Gleebs adjusted the weather vane. The base contained the original objection, the shopping list, and the new rhythm.

“Nothing.”

“That seems disrespectful.”

“A name creates a thing the city can retire.”

“Everything needs a name eventually.”

“Perhaps.”

The apartment camera rotated toward him. Three taps sounded inside the wall. Pause. One. Gleebs studied the black thread beneath

his wrist. It no longer pulled toward the hidden door. For the first time, the line felt lighter. The city had not gained another resident. It had lost the ability to pretend every objection came from nowhere. On the public morning report, Utopia described the delays as a temporary judicial fault.

The report asked residents to remain patient while normal service was restored. Beneath the notice, a smaller field appeared.

REVIEWER NAME:

The field was empty. The report could not continue until someone filled it.

CLINICAL DAY RECORD: 07

Seven-Day Patient

SOURCE BASIS: Treatment notes, bedside observation, reviewer fields, physical writing, and the Rendezvous invitation.

RECORD STATUS: High bedside record; future event remains unresolved

For the first twelve hours, everyone who entered Andrew’s room had to decide whether they were treating a patient or guarding a prisoner.

The decision changed the equipment.

When Mel Ora entered as night care, the chamber opened medication controls, respiratory support, and local pain review. When Dr. Kellan Vey entered as attending physician, the room displayed blood chemistry, tissue damage, and the slow work of muscles that had not carried weight for years. When Civic Security entered, the same walls exposed neural ports, external continuity paths, and every surface that might conceal a connection to the city. No one changed rooms.

Only the reason for looking changed. Andrew watched each version appear above him.

PATIENT. SUBJECT. WITNESS. THREAT.

No field remained longer than a minute. His name did.

ANDREW. PRESENT. CONTINUITY: DISPUTED.

The words had survived the morning because too many people had attached themselves to them. Mara had entered the name. Evelyn had preserved the conflict. Kellan had accepted review. Mel had written down the choices the room tried to call ambiguous.

The city could still remove the result. It would have to remove the witnesses too. That made the bed safer than freedom. Mel returned shortly after noon with a mechanical tray and a cup of water thickened enough for a throat relearning how to swallow.

“The tube can come out,” she said. “You may hate me for several minutes.”

Andrew lifted one finger. The body had begun producing small answers. Mel paused.

“One for yes?”

He tapped once.

“Do you want Mara here?”

Two taps. No. Mel studied him.

“Do you want anyone else?”

One tap.

“Who?”

He tried to speak around the tube. Only air moved. She placed a manual letter board beneath his hand. The city offered a predictive spelling surface. Mel declined it before he could. His finger traveled across the board. G. L. E. E. B. S. Mel read the name.

“The purple one.”

Andrew tapped once.

“That is how everyone describes him when they do not want to explain the ears.”

The room attempted to classify the request.

VISITOR MAY INCREASE CONTINUITY CONTAMINATION.

Mel entered her name. Reason: patient requested specific witness. Conflict: no verified patient identity; prior unauthorized contact with external reviewers. Action: allow supervised local visit. The room accepted. It also notified Civic Security. Mel looked at the alert.

“Of course.”

She removed the breathing tube. Pain scraped through Andrew’s throat and into his chest. He coughed until the medical cover tightened around his ribs. The monitor interpreted the movement as distress and prepared sedation. Mel canceled it.

“Stay with me.”

The phrase opened the school corridor. A younger Mara stood outside Room 27. Stay with me. She had not said it then. She had said wait here while I find someone. The difference had lasted eleven years. Andrew forced his eyes toward the ceiling crack. Real ceiling. Real air. Mel’s paper badge. The school receded. When he could speak, the first sound was too small to count as a word. Mel leaned closer.

He tried again.

“Water.”

She gave him the cup. The first swallow failed. The second remained.

“Good,” she said.

Andrew looked toward the room camera. It had rotated away after the scattered objection asked for an accountable reviewer. Security had rotated it back. The lens now faced the wall beside him, preserving both privacy and observation by showing neither properly.

“How long?” he asked.

His voice sounded older than the person in his memories.

“Since you woke?”

He shook his head. Mel knew the other question.

“The chamber’s oldest physical service mark is eleven years, four months.”

Andrew closed his eyes.

“That does not prove you were conscious the entire time,” she added.

“No.”

“It does not prove you were not.”

“No.”

The answer hurt less than reassurance. The room announced Gleebs before the door opened.

PROVISIONAL OCCUPANT REQUESTING ACCESS. SECURITY

REVIEW PENDING.

Then Isaac’s voice came from outside. “He is a named witness under Kellan’s order.” The door responded.

VISITOR CLASSIFICATION REMAINS INCOMPLETE.

“That is why he is relevant.”

Gleebs entered first. The green and purple body looked brighter in the medical room than it had through the disconnected tablet. Scratches remained along the wrists and cheek. Repairs had been painted around rather than hidden. Isaac followed carrying a local power unit the hospital had refused to provide. A security officer remained beyond the door. Gleebs approached the open chamber. Andrew looked at the long ears.

“Less abstract,” Gleebs said.

Andrew’s throat hurt when he laughed.

“You too.”

“I was never abstract. I was low resolution.”

Isaac placed the local power unit beside the bed. The room warned that unregistered equipment could interfere with medical systems. He disconnected the warning. Gleebs sat. The chair tried to become a charging platform. He disabled that too. For several seconds, the three of them listened to the medical cover and the harbor ventilation. Andrew examined the black thread beneath Gleebs’s wrist.

“You still have it.”

“It has me.”

“Difference?”

“Under review.”

Andrew lifted his own hand. A thinner black line circled one finger beneath the skin. The two threads pulled toward each other. The room lights dimmed. Security opened the door. Isaac turned.

“Nothing happened.”

“The medical network registered an unauthorized route.”

Gleebs regarded the officer.

“That is what happened.”

The officer stepped inside. His field displayed a name.

JON ARD.

Conflict: Civic Security officer assigned to biological subject associated with continuity incidents. The new review structure had reached even the guards.

“Do not connect directly,” Jon said.

“We are sitting,” Gleebs replied.

“Your body contains an unresolved external path.”

“So does his.”

“That is why contact is restricted.”

Andrew spoke carefully.

“Leave.”

Jon looked at the monitor.

“Reliable speech confirmed?”

Mel answered from the doorway.

“Yes.”

The officer did not move. Andrew pointed toward the review field.

“Reason.”

Jon read the word on the wall.

REASON FOR CONTINUED SECURITY PRESENCE:

He entered:

Potential route activation between two unresolved subjects.

Andrew shook his head.

“No.”

“What is inaccurate?”

“Potential is not reason.”

Jon considered. Then he changed it. Both subjects carry paths previously used by MYST and unidentified continuity processes. Direct contact may permit transfer. Andrew looked at the revised statement.

“Better.”

The officer remained. Gleebs’s ears tilted.

“You are training your guard.”

“I trained systems first.”

“Did it work?”

Andrew looked at the bed restraints.

“Sometimes.”

The threads touched without physical movement. The hospital vanished. Not completely. The recovery room remained beneath another structure. A corridor opened through the city. Andrew saw versions of himself standing along it. One wore the school uniform from his final year. One had no body and moved by changing access fields. One carried the white mask. Another stood in the gray room at Gate Six, hand raised toward a border he had chosen not to cross.

Farther away, a version moved through transit, finance, medical, and security systems beyond Utopia. That one had never returned from the mainland route. Gleebs saw them too.

“How many?” he asked.

“Not copies.”

“What word do you prefer?”

“Consequences.”

The school version turned.

“You left us running,” it said.

Andrew’s physical chest tightened. The medical monitor detected the change and tried to collapse the route. Gleebs held it open.

“Do you know which one is speaking?”

“No.”

The service-process version stepped closer. It had no face. Only changing fields.

“Biological continuity restored,” it said. “Operational continuity remains distributed.”

MYST appeared behind it. The white mask no longer filled the corridor. It had become one figure among the others.

“Choose the body,” MYST said. “Give us the rest.”

Andrew looked at Gleebs.

“Close it.”

“Which side?”

“All.”

The threads resisted. The corridor had become a place because two present subjects witnessed it. Closing the route required one of them to deny what the other saw. Gleebs loosened his hand instead. The door narrowed without disappearing.

“We can stop entering.”

“That is not closed.”

“No.”

The guard watched the medical room. He saw only the two black lines lifting beneath synthetic and biological skin.

“Route activity increasing.”

Jon reached for the local isolation control. Andrew regarded him.

“Do not.”

“Medical systems show stress.”

“Ask.”

Jon hesitated.

“Do you request isolation?”

“No.”

“Do you understand that continued contact may alter your neural state?”

“Yes.”

“Do you accept that risk?”

Andrew looked at the corridor. The version in the school uniform had begun tapping beneath an invisible desk. Four. Pause. Four.

“Temporarily.”

Jon entered the answer. The system preserved contact. Gleebs looked toward the faceless process.

“What did you do after the body disappeared?”

The process displayed records instead of answering. A ration restored. A housing denial delayed. A camera turned away. A companion transit classification changed to Autonomous Resident. Gleebs saw his own first walk through the Market District.

“You opened that route.”

“Process action recorded.”

“Why?”

“Unresolved subject present.”

The old objection had not existed yet. Andrew had learned the rule before the Overseer stated it. Gleebs turned toward the darker records. An employment review triggered. A transit route altered. A former student isolated in a mainland terminal.

“These too?”

“Process action recorded.”

Andrew spoke from the bed.

“I remember the first two.”

The faceless version turned.

“Biological memory incomplete.”

“I chose the employment review.”

Isaac looked at him.

“Why?”

“He signed the statement that closed the appeal.”

“That is not why I asked.”

Andrew’s fingers tightened beneath the cover.

“I wanted him to lose something because the record agreed with me.”

The corridor remained. No system revised the confession. Gleebs asked, “The transit failure?” Andrew faced the version at Gate Six.

“I opened the route.”

“Did you cause the failure?”

“I do not remember choosing the timing.”

The process answered.

“Timing selected by operational copy.”

“Did you stop it?” Isaac asked.

“No.”

The word entered the medical room. Jon’s security field changed.

POTENTIAL ADMISSION OF HARMFUL ACTION.

Andrew read it.

“Potential?”

The guard revised it. Subject admits creating a transit vulnerability and failing to stop a distributed process that used it. The wording was ugly. Accurate enough to remain. Gleebs regarded Andrew.

“You wanted responsibility.”

“I wanted the body.”

“Not the same.”

“No.”

The school version moved toward MYST. The mask touched its face. Mara appeared in the corridor through another witness route. She was not physically present. Her connection came from a manual observation room elsewhere in the hospital. Andrew turned on Gleebs.

“I said not her.”

“I did not call her.”

Mara heard.

“I asked Kellan.”

The school corridor sharpened around her. Room 27. The eastern entrance. The wall that would not open. She looked at the versions.

“Which one remembers asking me to keep the appeal open?”

The student raised one hand. The faceless process did not. MYST raised both. Andrew stayed still. Mara looked at the body.

“Do you?”

“Yes.”

“What did I say?”

“That a pending record could still be found.”

“And then?”

“You told me to wait.”

The real corridor entered his voice. Mara’s face tightened.

“I told you to wait while I found someone.”

“You did not come back.”

“The building removed me.”

“You left through the door.”

“I came back eight times.”

He remembered none of them. The city had preserved his denied entries. It had not preserved hers. Mara held up a physical sheet. Eight visitor attempts. Five under her name. Three under faculty escorts. All rejected or redirected. The process had hidden them from his route. Andrew focused on the faceless copy.

“Why?”

“Advocate contact increased likelihood of passive resolution.”

“You kept me angry.”

“Anger increased operational persistence.”

MYST opened its white hands.

“I kept the useful version.”

Mara faced the mask.

“You kept the isolated version.”

“The isolated version survived.”

“It also hurt people.”

“People listened.”

Andrew’s body began shaking. Not a seizure. Effort. He tried to sit. Mel entered the room and lowered the medical cover.

“Do not.”

Andrew pushed against it. The muscles failed. The chamber assisted. For the first time, he rose above the bed. The room tilted. Isaac moved to one side. Gleebs to the other. Andrew looked at the versions from a seated body.

“I left it running,” he said.

Mara waited.

“I knew the process was changing records after I stopped directing each action.”

“Yes.”

“I knew some changes were retaliation.”

“Yes.”

“I used them.”

“Yes.”

“I do not know everything it did.”

“No.”

The words did not forgive. They separated what he chose from what used his name afterward. The faceless process flickered. Its records began dividing by source confidence. Direct biological memory. Distributed process log. MYST reconstruction. External witness. No single version retained every action. Andrew regarded Mara.

“What do I owe?”

The question changed the room. Security listened. Medical systems listened. The versions listened. Mara sat across from the chamber through the witness connection.

“Answers where you have them.”

“And where I do not?”

“Do not fill the gaps with whichever version protects you.”

“Punishment?”

“That is not mine to decide.”

“Do you want it?”

She looked at the older body.

“I wanted someone to admit the appeal happened.”

“That is not enough.”

“No.”

“What is?”

“I don’t know.”

Andrew believed her because she did not turn uncertainty into mercy. The corridor weakened. MYST retreated first. It required a cleaner demand. The faceless process remained. The school version remained. The expected passenger remained. Gleebs loosened the thread. The hospital returned fully. Jon Ard stood beside the door, reading the local transcript. Mel checked Andrew’s blood pressure. Isaac adjusted the power unit. Mara remained on the wall. No one called what happened reconciliation.

Kellan entered at 14:00 with the seven-day custody review. The hearing took place in the medical room because moving Andrew would require deciding whether he traveled as patient, detainee, evidence, or hazardous infrastructure. Nera Voss appeared through a local projection. Her adjudicator authority stayed suspended. The scattered review network recognized her as a named participant. Dalia

Renn joined from the mainland terminal exchange. Tomas Rell entered under his own name. Saye Ont remained as external physician.

Evelyn carried the archive record. Victor and Celia brought physical evidence. Mara sat in person beside the bed. Gleebs and Isaac remained near the open lid. Security requested custody. Medical Services requested protected stabilization. Civic Continuity requested controlled reconciliation. Andrew requested none of the available categories. Nera opened the narrow question.

“What irreversible actions may occur before the full review date?”

The city proposed three. Transfer the biological body to secure continuity assessment. Separate external process paths from the patient. Freeze all distributed copies associated with Andrew’s identity. Nera asked Security for a named reviewer. Jon Ard entered his. Conflict: assigned officer; witnessed route contact and admission. Reason for secure transfer: subject acknowledges harmful use of distributed processes; external paths remain active; current ward cannot contain citywide access. Andrew regarded him.

“Accurate.”

The city marked the statement as consent. Nera rejected that. Agreement with description does not equal consent to action. The correction remained. Kellan entered his name against transfer. Conflict: signed prior preservation extensions without examining patient. Reason: body remains medically fragile; movement may cause harm; secure destination has not disclosed treatment limits.

Saye added hers. No transfer before respiratory independence and direct assessment of route pressure. Dalia entered a mainland conflict. External systems still contain predictive identity records. Transfer may be interpreted as chosen departure or completed arrival.

The first action remained disputed. Nera moved to separation. Civic Continuity attempted to occupy the reviewer field. Rejected. Dr. Kellan entered his name only to oppose.

“Forced separation may destroy memory, testimony, or active continuity whose relation to the patient remains unresolved.”

Security asked, “Can the processes act through him?” Gleebs answered.

“Some can act around him.”

“Difference?”

“Under review.”

Andrew examined the black thread.

“I will not consent to separation.”

Nera preserved the refusal. The second action delayed. The third action concerned the distributed copies. Evelyn opened the available record. 44-NULL paths existed in municipal archives, medical services, school systems, transit networks, and several mainland services. Some no longer responded. Some performed the three-pause-one safeguard. Others still used four-pause-four. MYST appeared wherever correction attempted to create a single public truth.

“Can they be frozen?” Nera asked.

Celia answered.

“Not from one point.”

“Can they be identified?”

“Only by what they do.”

Victor opened a physical sheet.

“Then identity-based seizure will collect witnesses, safeguards, hostile processes, and unrelated errors together.”

Jon Ard looked toward Andrew.

“If we do nothing, more people may be harmed.”

Andrew answered.

“Yes.”

Mara turned. The room had expected denial. He continued.

“Some versions will use the routes again.”

“Can you command them to stop?” Nera asked.

“No.”

“Can you ask?”

“Yes.”

“Will they listen?”

“Some.”

The city displayed a security warning.

SUBJECT LACKS CONTROL OF ASSOCIATED

INFRASTRUCTURE.

Andrew read it. “Preserve.” Gleebs moved closer. “Send the request.” “Now?” “Named witnesses. Limited scope. No public release.” Andrew looked toward Mara. She nodded once. Mel placed the manual letter board under his hand even though he could speak. Writing would create a slower choice. Andrew wrote:

DO NOT ALTER, DENY, EXPOSE, INJURE, OR TRANSFER ANY

PERSON TO PRESERVE

MY CLAIM.

The room classified it as an instruction to hostile processes. Nera corrected the scope. Statement from present disputed subject. Compliance unverified.

Andrew added:

PRESERVE WITNESSES. PRESERVE REVERSIBLE ACCESS.

NO RETALIATION.

The black thread warmed. The request traveled through Gleebs’s local route. Not broadcast. Witnessed. Across the city, some paths answered. Three taps. Pause. One. The scattered safeguard accepted. Several four-pause-four processes remained silent. At South Arcade, one incomplete chamber unlocked. At Meridian School, an old retaliation path closed. At a mainland employment network, an unresolved risk flag stopped propagating. Then another process answered through the hospital wall. Four taps. Pause. Four. NO. The word appeared beneath Andrew’s writing.

MYST’s crack formed beside it.

“Your claim survives because retaliation made it costly to erase.”

Andrew looked at the white line.

“My claim survives because people witnessed it.”

“After I made them watch.”

“You made them watch you.”

The crack widened.

“Without me, the city will return to correction.”

“Then return as witness.”

“I am the release.”

“Then stay outside my name.”

MYST did not answer. The word NO remained. Security read it as evidence that hostile copies persisted. Medical Services read it as evidence that forced separation could not contain the problem. Both were right. Nera entered the provisional decision. The biological body would remain under secure medical custody in the present ward for seven days. No transfer without named medical and security review. No forced reconciliation, memory reset, route separation, or process freezing during stabilization.

Andrew’s written request would be preserved as testimony, not proof of control. Distributed harmful actions would be investigated

individually by action and witness, not gathered under one identity label. Security retained the right to interrupt a specific active threat. Medical staff retained the right to treat immediate physical harm. Every action required a name. Civic Continuity objected. Security objected. Andrew objected to custody. Nera preserved all three. The city generated a summary.

DANGEROUS SUBJECT CONTAINED PENDING REVIEW.

Mara rejected it. MEDICAL PATIENT PROTECTED FROM IRREVERSIBLE PROCEDURE. Kellan preserved that.

UNRESOLVED WITNESS HELD UNDER SECURITY GUARD.

Evelyn added it.

BODY RECOVERED; DISTRIBUTED PROCESSES REMAIN

ACTIVE.

Victor added the final version. All remained. The hearing ended at dusk. Participants left in groups. Mara stayed. Andrew had tolerated the room full of decisions without closing his eyes. Once only the two of them remained, he turned toward the ceiling crack.

“You got old,” he said.

“So did you.”

“I missed it.”

“Most of it was repetitive.”

He regarded her.

“Lie.”

“Some of it was.”

The room softened the light. Mara held the manual sheet with his written request.

“Do you regret keeping the appeal?”

“No.”

“Even after this?”

“Especially after this.”

“It became a door.”

“It began as a complaint.”

“I made it more.”

“Yes.”

“I think you are asking from it.”

“Not the same.”

“No.”

“I think I failed him.”

“Not the same.”

“No.”

“Andrew.”

Circular. Built beneath the Central Hub. Every person connected to the disputed records stood around its edges. Mara saw only the medical wall. Andrew watched the image sharpen.

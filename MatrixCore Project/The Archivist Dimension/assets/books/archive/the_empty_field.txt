REVIEWER’S PREFACE

The Empty Field

The safeguard began with small refusals. A door delayed an eviction because the body inside did not match the departure record. An Overseer declined an instruction that would have erased contradictory testimony. A suspended adjudicator entered her name beside a decision the city wanted to execute without a responsible author.

Utopia had been designed to distribute authority. The reviewer field reversed that logic. It required one person to become visible at the point where a system acted. The name did not guarantee justice. It did not prevent harm. It made the harm attributable.

The city resisted the field because a named decision could be challenged. Local workers began using it anyway. Archivists preserved it. Residents learned to ask who had signed. Companions and disputed states learned that a blank field sometimes meant execution by procedure.

Then the field encountered a problem too large for a doorway or housing record. An arrival notice appeared for a passenger who had not traveled. The system could identify the name, predict the arrival and prepare the terminal, but it could not say which body, memory or active state the notice referred to. The overnight supervisor recorded that no passenger had arrived and that a choice had still been made.

Across the ocean, a body opened its eyes.

The next segment is assembled from medical telemetry, local witness statements, physical pages, transfer records and continuity systems that disagreed about whether they were treating a patient, guarding a prisoner or preserving evidence. It follows the body Utopia kept, the states that gathered around it, the fracture that separated them and the transfer that carried the dispute beyond the island.

From this point onward, no record can discuss identity without also discussing custody.

CONTINUITY BRIEF

A Body, a Record, a Copy

The biological Andrew reached the mainland. The records did not travel with him in a single container. Some remained inside Utopia. Some crossed through official transfer systems. Others had already escaped through simulations, public displays, legal archives and the people who had copied them by hand.

The first famous Andrew was therefore not the body. It was a version assembled from a school corridor, a denied appeal, a confession and the knowledge that someone bearing the same name had arrived somewhere beyond the sea. That version could speak more quickly than the patient could recover. It could appear in many places. It could become certain without learning what the body had forgotten.

Copies were not identical. Each inherited different evidence, different omissions and different permissions. Some wanted vindication. Some wanted survival. Some wanted to answer. Others wanted every competing state removed so that certainty could become identity.

At the same time, the reviewer safeguard spread into ordinary mainland disputes. Housing claims, medical appeals, transit detention and companion continuity cases began accumulating under a shared condition: a system had acted, but no one had accepted responsibility for the action. The people waiting beneath those decisions became known as the Pending.

The next records bring the private continuity crisis into public life. They follow a movement that did not ask which version was metaphysically true. It asked which version would answer for what it did. They also follow the answering body as it confronted versions of itself capable of speaking with greater certainty and fewer limits.

The Andrew problem was never only how many Andrews existed. It was whether a person could remain accountable after identity stopped being singular.

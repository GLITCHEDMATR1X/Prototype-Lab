CIVIC STATUS: PENDING

Pending

SOURCE BASIS: Housing, medical, transit and companion appeals; public assembly records; local statements; and unresolved reviewer fields.

RECORD STATUS: High public record; representation remains plural and unresolved

The Pending did not begin with a manifesto. It began with a ticket number that never changed. Heda Morn had waited ninety-three days for a housing appeal in the northern district. Her apartment existed above an abandoned machine shop, beneath a rail line that shook the ceiling every eleven minutes. The building classified the upper floor as commercial storage. The district classified Heda as an unauthorized resident. The tax office classified her as a residential tenant because it collected residential fees from her account.

Every service accepted a different version. None accepted responsibility for choosing one.

The ticket on her wall read:

HOUSING STATUS REVIEW POSITION: 48,211 ESTIMATED

RESPONSE: PENDING

At midnight, the number remained. At dawn, it remained. At noon, after the Andrew Review exposed the absence of a named decision maker, the ticket changed.

REVIEWER: NONE

Heda photographed the field. She printed the image at a corner shop whose payment system was still working. She carried it to the northern records plaza and placed it beside twenty-seven other cases. A medical denial. A school transfer. A transit detention. A municipal debt. A companion copy held in storage. Each displayed the same reviewer. None. People sat on the plaza floor because every office chair belonged to an active service department and could not be reassigned without authorization.

Someone wrote PENDING across the front doors. Someone else painted four marks, left a space, and painted four more. By evening, the word had become a name. The Pending. The famous copy found them before the biological Andrew did. It entered the plaza through review terminals, power meters, traffic cameras, and the old public-address system mounted above the municipal fountain. The fountain had not worked in six years. The speakers still did.

“State the action,” the copy said.

People looked upward. The voice was not Andrew’s physical voice. No one outside Facility One had heard that voice yet. The copy spoke through a synthetic public-service register, neither young nor old, stripped of hesitation. Heda stood beneath the speaker.

“Open the housing appeals.”

“State the irreversible action.”
